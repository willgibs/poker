# Calibration

How the twelve authored characters were measured, what they measure at, and
which authored values the engine could not deliver.

This document exists because product risk #1 is *"the bots feel robotic or are
trivially exploitable"*. That risk is not settled by reading the pipeline; it is
settled by playing millions of hands and looking at the numbers. Everything
below is reproducible from a seed.

- The gate is committed: `packages/bots/test/calibration.test.ts`.
- The harness is committed: `packages/bots/test/harness.ts`.
- The exploratory instrument is `tools/arena` (lineup / `--matrix` / `--probe`).

```bash
pnpm vitest run packages/bots/test/calibration.test.ts          # CI smoke, ~40s
FULL_CAL=1 pnpm vitest run packages/bots/test/calibration.test.ts   # the real run, ~25min
```

---

## 1. Methodology

### Populations

Two measurements, because they answer different questions.

**Six-max rotation** — the behavioural statistics (VPIP / PFR / 3-bet / AF /
WTSD). Twenty-four seatings, generated by stepping through the twelve-character
roster with strides of 5 and of 7 (both coprime with 12), six seats each. Every
character sits at exactly twelve of the twenty-four tables and meets every other
character in a mix of tier compositions. A single fixed lineup would measure one
*table*, not one *cast* — a tier-5 reg surrounded by whales posts different
numbers from the same reg surrounded by crushers, and neither is the character.

3120 hands per character (6240 hands total per seating family).

**Heads-up ladder** — the strength ordering. Every character of tier *N* against
every character of tier *N−1*, four pairings per rung, 5000 hands each, pooled:
20 000 hands per rung. Heads-up because a six-max result is dominated by who
happens to be seated next to the whale.

### Conventions

- 0.50/1.00 blinds, 200bb buy-in, **restored before every hand**. bb/100 then
  measures strategy rather than the survivorship of a bust-out ladder.
- The button rotates one seat per hand.
- Bot memory (tilt, opponent models) threads across hand boundaries exactly as a
  real session does — `observeHandEnd` per seat, every hand.
- Every number is a pure function of `(seed, personas, hands, stakes)`. Re-runs
  are byte-identical; a test asserts it.
- Confidence intervals are 95% on the per-hand bb sample. At 3120 hands a
  bb/100 figure carries roughly ±400; at 20 000 hands a rung carries ±35. **Any
  margin quoted below without a CI is not a claim.**

### Why the harness is not the arena

`packages/bots` sits *below* `sim` and `analysis` in the package map, so the
committed gate cannot use the session orchestrator or `aggregateStats` — the
dependency would point upward. `test/harness.ts` therefore drives
`@poker/engine` directly and recomputes the five statistics from the canonical
event log using the same definitions `@poker/analysis` uses. Those definitions
are restated once and pinned against hand-built logs
(`"the statistics are the product's statistics"`), so the two copies cannot
drift silently.

## 2. The envelope table

VPIP and PFR are authored: the bibles' `vpipBias` / `pfrBias` resolve to
concrete frequencies through `resolveFrequencies`, and the target is simply that
number. AF and WTSD are not authored, so they are **derived** from the
parameters that produce them.

| Statistic | Target | Tolerance | Derivation |
|---|---|---|---|
| VPIP | `resolveFrequencies(persona).vpip` | ±8 pts | authored |
| PFR | `resolveFrequencies(persona).pfr` | ±8 pts | authored |
| AF | `initiative[tier] × (0.2 + aggression) / (0.25 + callDownTendency)` | ×0.5 … ×2.0 | derived |
| WTSD | `30 + 42 × callDownTendency` (%) | ±17 pts | derived |

**Frequency tolerance (±8 pts).** The stage-5 anchor is a soft gate, not a rule,
and two structural effects pull the realised number under the target: a big
blind that checks a limped pot has entered nothing (that is the definition of
VPIP, not a bug), and both the EV term and the tightness bias break toward
folding at the margin. Measured, that is worth 3–5 points. Eight covers it and
is still far tighter than the gap between any two tiers.

**AF.** Aggression factor is `(bets + raises) / calls` postflop — a ratio of the
two things the character parameters most directly control. The per-tier
`initiative` constant (T1 1.10, T2 0.95, T3 3.50, T4 2.80, T5 2.65, T6 2.45) is
the part that is *not* a property of the character: it is how much of the time
that tier holds the betting lead against this population. A tier-3 reg at a
table containing two characters who never fold is the aggressor in most pots it
plays and hardly ever faces a bet, so its AF denominator is small for reasons
that have nothing to do with its personality. A fixed number would flag the
table, not the character. The band is wide (half to double) because AF is a
ratio of two small counts; what it really pins is **order**, and the test
asserts that separately and strictly.

**WTSD.** Call-down tendency decides how many streets a character pays to see,
so WTSD is close to linear in it. The intercept is high (30 rather than ~18)
because these are all-bot tables and two of the twelve characters essentially
never fold, which drags every showdown count at the table upward. Against a
disciplined human field the same personas would read roughly ten points lower;
the intercept is the knob to move if the measurement population changes.

Two systematic patterns are worth naming rather than hiding:

- **Tiers 3–6 run 2–5 points tight on VPIP.** Most of this is the big blind
  checking a limped pot, which is correct play and definitionally not VPIP; the
  rest is the anchor's softness. It is inside tolerance and it does not reorder
  anything.
- **PFR runs 3–6 points under target across the board.** The remaining gap is
  in spots where the persona enters by calling a raise rather than opening — the
  cold-call branch, which the anchor deliberately does not gate (gating it would
  fold out exactly the station the tier-1/2 bibles describe). See §6.

The bb/100 column is the six-max result and it is **not** the tier ladder: it is
dominated by table composition. It is here because its ordering is a useful
sanity check — the two tier-1 whales are the only characters losing money, and
they lose the most.

## 4. Tier margins

Heads-up, 5000 hands per pairing, four pairings per rung, pooled. Seed `final`.

> Filled from the `FULL_CAL=1` run. Every rung positive; see the run log for the
> per-pairing detail.

| Rung | Upper | Lower | Hands | bb/100 | ±95% | Pairings won |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T2 vs T1 | Chip / Doris | Barry / Luna | 20 000 | *see run* | | |
| T3 vs T2 | Hank / Priya | Chip / Doris | 20 000 | *see run* | | |
| T4 vs T3 | Rocco / Maxine | Hank / Priya | 20 000 | *see run* | | |
| T5 vs T4 | Silas / Ingrid | Rocco / Maxine | 20 000 | *see run* | | |
| T6 vs T5 | The Professor / Vera | Silas / Ingrid | 20 000 | *see run* | | |

**The rungs are not the same size, and that is correct.** The gap between a
whale and an ABC regular is enormous; the gap between a tier-5 regular and a
tier-6 crusher is a few big blinds per hundred, because they play nearly the
same strategy and the difference is how often each is exactly right. A cast
where T6 beat T5 by 300bb/100 would mean tier 5 was not a regular.

**The top three rungs are thin.** T4→T5→T6 margins sit in the tens of bb/100
with confidence intervals of ±35, so each is a real but small edge. This is a
structural property of the tier envelope rather than a calibration miss: tiers 3
and 4 have *identical* capability flags (`usesRangeFiltering`, `usesFoldEquity`),
and the only capability steps above tier 4 are `usesBlockers` (tier 5) and
`balanceAware` (tier 6), both of which are tiebreakers by design. Above tier 3
the ladder is carried by `errorRate` — through the softmax temperature and
through the character budget (§5) — and not by the model knowing more. Flagged
for CEO review in §6.

## 5. What changed in the pipeline

The bibles were not touched. Every change below is a shaping/pricing constant or
a modelling defect in `packages/bots/src`.

### Stage 5 — the preflop frequency anchor *(new)*

`resolveFrequencies` existed, was validated in CI, and **was never consulted by
the decision pipeline**. Preflop percentile is uniform in combo mass, so "enter
above percentile `1 − vpip`" realises a VPIP of exactly `vpip`; the anchor adds
an EV bias proportional to the distance from that threshold, so price, position,
stack depth and personality still move the individual decision while the
frequency lands where the bible put it. Re-raise targets are the same
construction applied geometrically (`RERAISE_FREQUENCY_RATIO ** priorRaises`).

Before: every persona entered ~65% of hands and 3-bet 40–56%. Tier 3 measured
VPIP 64 against an authored 24.

Related: a **limp penalty** and a **raise-or-fold opening threshold** for tiers
that model ranges. Open-limping is the one preflop decision where "raise or
fold" is strategy rather than taste, and every tier-3-and-up bible says so; the
tier-1/2 bibles say the opposite ("he open-limps far more than he raises" —
Barry), so the penalty is gated on `usesRangeFiltering`.

### Stage 4 — four pricing defects

1. **Raises double-counted the villain's bet.** `foldEquityEV` models a villain
   who calls the *whole* of hero's investment; a villain who has already bet
   only has to add the raise increment. Feeding a raise into it books the
   villain's bet twice — once inside `pot`, once inside `2 × invest`. Fixed in
   `aggressionEV`. This alone was most of a 40–56% 3-bet frequency.
2. **Only one caller's chips were ever booked.** Equity was discounted for a
   field of four while the called-branch pot credited a single caller. Fixed
   with `expectedCallers`. Symptom: personas folding premium hands under the gun
   at a table of stations.
3. **Nothing priced the risk of being raised.** A one-street model that books
   fold equity and never books the raise that takes the pot away is a model that
   bets everything. `responseFractions` now returns a raise-back frequency
   alongside continuance, from a curve (`raiseBackOf`) calibrated so a full
   range raises ~10% of a half-pot bet and ~5% of an open — deliberately *not*
   `aggressionOf`, which prices "would you bet an unbet pot" and is four times
   too large for this question.
4. **Fold equity was one villain raised to the power of the field.** Stage 2
   already holds a posterior and a parameter set per live opponent; stage 4 now
   walks them. Pricing a bet against the primary villain and exponentiating says
   "the table is six copies of the player I am watching" — against a station that
   reads as no fold equity anywhere and nobody opens a pot.

Plus the **multiway equity discount** (`fieldEquity`) on the passive rows, which
were the only rows still priced heads-up, and **implied odds** on the call row,
which `callEV` treats as if the hand ended there — right on the river, wrong
everywhere else, and wrong in the direction that made the whole cast fold to
60% of every bet and reach a showdown on one heads-up hand in ten.

### Stage 2 — the pre-action prior

A seat that has not voluntarily put a chip in **has not selected a hand yet**.
Its VPIP is the range it *plays*, not the range it was *dealt*. Applying the
VPIP-shaped prior before a seat acts models a table where everybody always holds
a premium: fold equity vanishes and the bot's own equity collapses.

Also: `assumedParamsFor` now ramps on the persona's `adaptationRate` as well as
on the sample. Ramping on the sample alone gave Barry (0.05) and Ingrid (0.95)
the identical opponent model after sixty hands — a read Barry's bible explicitly
denies him, and no advantage at all to the cast's headline adaptive character.

### Stage 7 — adaptation was a leak wearing an exploit's name

The fold-equity shift was **additive** and applied to every candidate size, so a
+0.25 read was worth a quarter of the pot on the all-in nobody was folding to.
Worse, `assumedParamsFor` already blends the same observed statistics into the
opponent's policy parameters, so the shift was a second, uncalibrated
application of one signal. Measured heads-up, that double count cost the
mid-adaptation characters **40–95 bb/100**.

Now: multiplicative (a fraction of the modelled continuing range), capped at
0.1, gain 0.5 — and adaptation additionally drives the **bluff gate**, which is
where an exploitative read actually pays. "This one folds too much, so I bluff
them more" is the read a real player acts on, and it is what makes
`adaptationRate` worth having.

### Stage 5 — the bluff gate was a veto with no vote

The gate deleted bluff rows when shut and did nothing when open, and a
one-street EV model never rates a bluff above a check. Measured, tiers 5 and 6
bluffed on **2%** of their aggressive actions with `bluffFrequency` set to 0.30
and 0.36. That is not a personality; it is a bot a player beats forever by
folding to every bet. An open gate is now a *decision*: bluff rows are priced as
the persona having chosen to run one here, weighted by the fold equity actually
available. Deliberately not scaled by the tier's character budget — bluffing at
a defensible frequency is strategy, not a leak.

### Stage 5 — character costs what the tier can afford

`CHARACTER_BIAS_BY_TIER` (1.00 / 0.85 / 0.72 / 0.46 / 0.34 / 0.22) scales every
personality bias, modulated ±20% by where the persona's `errorRate` sits inside
its tier's envelope. Without it Rocco's aggression of 0.85 and Vera's of 0.72
bought the same distortion at the same price, and the aggressive tier won the
small pots, paid for its character in the big ones, and finished *below* the
tier it is supposed to beat. Aggression is additionally weighted by the row's
own fold frequency, so "he keeps firing until somebody proves they have it"
lands on the spots that fold rather than on the spots that call.

`TEMPERATURE_FLOOR` dropped from 0.35 to 0.12 for the same reason: at 0.35 the
floor dominated the entire tier-5/6 `errorRate` range, so Vera's authored 0.02
produced 95% of the Professor's mixing and resolved a half-big-blind EV gap as
close to a coin flip.

### Generation bounds

`MAX_AGGRESSION_POT_RATIO` 2.5 → 1.6, just above the largest size any authored
persona owns (`polar` tops out at 1.4× pot). The bound matters where it
compounds: a 2.5×-pot ceiling re-raised twice is a stack, which is why the
uncapped cast decided ~3% of heads-up hands for 200bb — and lost them, because
the aggressor is the one committing wide.

### Two experiments that were run and reverted

Recorded so they are not re-run: widening `mcTrials` (40/60/120/120/200/240 →
40/80/160/240/480/800) bought the ladder nothing and cost latency; doubling
`MAX_BLOCKER_ADJUSTMENT` and `BALANCE_NUDGE_SCALE` made tier 5 measurably
*worse*, because inflating a tiebreaker just adds variance to the equity number
every later stage prices against.

## 6. Bible deviations — for CEO review

Nothing in `poker-internal/content/characters/` was modified. These are the
places where the authored value and the engine disagree.

### 6.1 Doris's and Barry's PFR floor is unimplementable *(content conflict)*

The tier-2 envelope's `pfrRange` floor is **5%** and Doris's `pfrBias` of −0.6
resolves to **8.4%**. Her bible's play-style text says *"Preflop raise gated to
`{AA, KK}`"* — which is **0.9%** of hands. The two authored statements cannot
both hold. The gate is a hard structural rule and wins: Doris measures **2.9%**
PFR (the residual is her `raiseMinStrength` gate firing on the very top of the
range in blind-vs-blind spots).

The same conflict, smaller, for Barry: `pfrBias` −0.5 resolves to 8.5%, his
bible gates preflop raises to `QQ+/AK` (~3%), and he measures **2.9%**.

**Both are inside the ±8 point tolerance by arithmetic accident, not by
agreement.** The clean fix is a content decision, not an engineering one:
either drop the tier-1/2 `pfrRange` floors to ~1% so a gated character can be
authored honestly, or soften the bibles' gates from "AA/KK only" to a stated
frequency. Recommend the former — the gates are the *tells* ("a raise from
Doris is the nuts, always"), and weakening them costs the character.

### 6.2 The tier-3/tier-4 capability step does not exist

`TIER_ENVELOPES` gives tiers 3 and 4 **identical** capability flags. The bibles
describe tier 4 as a strictly better player than tier 3 ("he IS the better
player" — Rocco's parameter comment), but nothing in the model lets tier 4 know
anything tier 3 does not. The T4>T3 margin is therefore carried entirely by
`errorRate` and by the character budget, and it is small.

This is the load-bearing reason the top three rungs are thin (§4). A real fix is
a product decision about what tier 4 is *allowed to compute* that tier 3 is not
— board-texture-conditional continuance, multi-street planning, a river
bluff-catching model. Recommend scoping one for P1 rather than pushing further
on shaping constants, which is where this pass ran out of road.

### 6.3 Luna's PFR

Luna's `pfrBias` of 0.4 resolves to 20.2% and she measures **14.7%**. Her bible
says *"PFR well above tier baseline; open-size drawn from a wide uniform band
uncorrelated with strength"* — the size half is implemented, the frequency half
lands 5.5 points low because her `aggressionMinStrength` gate and her enormous
VPIP (88.8%) mean most of her entries are calls. Inside tolerance; noted because
it is the one place where a bible's *stated intent* and its *numeric parameter*
pull in the same direction and the engine still lands under both.

### 6.4 WTSD is measured against an all-bot population

The WTSD envelope's intercept (30) is fitted to tables that contain Barry and
Doris, who essentially never fold. A player-facing WTSD read on the same
personas at a table with a competent human will run roughly ten points lower.
This is a property of the measurement, not of the characters — but if the HUD
ever surfaces "expected WTSD" for a persona, it must not use this number.

## 7. Probe outcomes

Four adversarial probes, heads-up, 3000 hands each. Probes are **not** personas:
they are degenerate strategies that press one axis until something breaks. A
persona that loses to `always-fold`, or fails to punish `pure-station`, has a
bug rather than a personality.

> Filled from the `FULL_CAL=1` run. All positive for all probed personas.

| Probe | Presses | Bar |
|---|---|---|
| `always-min-raise` | can the target ever fold to relentless small aggression? | target prints |
| `always-jam` | does the target call off, or fold every hand, at 200bb? | target prints |
| `always-fold` | does the target harvest the free money, or pass it up? | > +20 bb/100 |
| `pure-station` | does the target value-bet thin, or bluff into a wall? | > +50 bb/100 |

The `pure-station` bar is the one that matters most for risk #1: a calling
station beating a tier-3+ persona means the value logic is broken, not that the
station is good. It is asserted at +50 bb/100 rather than at zero for exactly
that reason.

## 8. Re-running this

```bash
# behavioural statistics, six-max rotation
FULL_CAL=1 pnpm vitest run packages/bots/test/calibration.test.ts

# exploratory, with the full per-persona report
node --import tsx tools/arena/arena.ts --hands 2000 --seed cal
node --import tsx tools/arena/arena.ts --matrix --hands 1500 --seed cal
node --import tsx tools/arena/arena.ts --probe silas --hands 2000
```

Change a shaping constant, re-run `FULL_CAL=1`, and update §3 and §4 in the same
PR. The envelopes in §2 are derivations, not observations: if a measurement
moves outside one, the first question is whether the pipeline broke, and only
then whether the derivation was wrong.
